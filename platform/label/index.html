Data Layer

Label

AI annotates. Humans verify. Quality is measured, not assumed.

What It Does

Label is the layer where raw signal acquires meaning. A multi-engine orchestration system dispatches every data point to the model best suited to its modality and task, while a calibrated confidence threshold discerns which annotations the machine may certify outright and which demand human judgment. Nothing leaves unverified — each label carries either the AI's high-confidence assertion or a reviewer's explicit sign-off, the twin foundations of data a model can actually be trained on.

How Routing Works

Every item is scored — a confidence value between 0.00 and 0.99, issued by the engine that labeled it. Cross the configured threshold (0.85, by default) and the label stands on its own; fall short, and the item is queued for a human eye. That threshold bends to the dataset: loosen it for throughput, tighten it for precision. Three numbers tell you how the balance is holding — the auto-label rate, the human review rate, and the correction rate, the share of AI labels a reviewer chose to overrule.

The AI Engines

Four specialised model families route each item to the right kind of intelligence — text, vision, video, and audio — each producing its own confidence score.

Human Review Interface
Human judgment finishes what confidence can't: a keyboard-driven review stream surfaces the AI's suggestion, and a reviewer accepts, corrects, or rejects it in three to five seconds — a fraction of conventional tools' fifteen to thirty. Error tags apply with a single keystroke.
Quality Measurement
Quality is measured, not asserted. Every run yields a verified agreement rate, confidence calibration, label distribution, and a composite readiness score — one number worth trusting. Each metric travels with the dataset into the version's lineage report.
